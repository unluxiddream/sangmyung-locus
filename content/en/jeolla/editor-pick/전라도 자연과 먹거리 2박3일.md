# From North to South: A 2-Night, 3-Day Trip Through Jeolla, Following Nature and the Dinner Table

This is a record of my 2-night, 3-day trip through Jeolla.  
I don't normally run a travel blog, so the writing may come across as a bit stiff. Thank you for your understanding...

The theme of this trip is "nature" and "food." See nature, eat, see nature again, eat again... Simple, isn't it?

- **DAY 1 (Jeonbuk / North Jeolla)** Buan → Gunsan → Jeonju
- **DAY 2 (Jeonbuk → Jeonnam)** Jeonju → Imsil → Sunchang → Damyang → Naju
- **DAY 3 (Jeonnam / South Jeolla)** Suncheon → Yeosu

Many of the places I wanted to visit are closed on Mondays, so I scheduled the trip from Friday to Sunday. 

## [DAY 1] From Cliffs Stacked Like Bookshelves to a Kettle of Makgeolli

Chaeseokgang → Isungdang → Chowon Photo Studio → Sinheung-dong Japanese-style House → Gyeongam-dong Railroad Village → Samcheon-dong Makgeolli Alley

### Chaeseokgang (채석강) | The First Page of the Trip Is 70-Million-Year-Old Strata

Our first stop is Chaeseokgang in Buan.  
It is a stretch of coastal cliffs and rock shelf running about 1.5 km from the right side of Gyeokpo Port to Dakibong Peak. The horizontally layered strata look like a stack of books. They are said to be the Gyeokpo Formation, created in the Cretaceous period of the Mesozoic Era about 70 million years ago. 

![https://i.namu.wiki/i/WN9WSDn0jRg-HZx96AQNr3pQp-wViyejCR0ocD5p7yKngGqi3NggT7GqZbSVejPQkNb_3GL0MBHxuuMCJq3pGA.webp](https://i.namu.wiki/i/WN9WSDn0jRg-HZx96AQNr3pQp-wViyejCR0ocD5p7yKngGqi3NggT7GqZbSVejPQkNb_3GL0MBHxuuMCJq3pGA.webp)

Isn't it magnificent?

![https://imgnn.seoul.co.kr/img/upload/2026/06/19/SSC_20260619143801_V.jpg.webp](https://imgnn.seoul.co.kr/img/upload/2026/06/19/SSC_20260619143801_V.jpg.webp)

The sea caves at the foot of the cliffs can only be viewed up close at low tide, so I checked the tide table before setting out. I heard it is truly beautiful at sunset, but since we came in the morning, I couldn't see it. 

![[채석강]]

### Isungdang (이성당) | Lunch at the Oldest Bakery in Korea

It took about an hour and twenty minutes by car from Buan to Gunsan.  
If you come to Gunsan, you absolutely have to stop by Isungdang! It is known as the oldest existing bakery in Korea, running since 1945. They say it began as a wagashi (Japanese confectionery) shop called "Izumoya," run by a Japanese owner during the colonial period, which a Korean took over after liberation and renamed to its current name.

![https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAxOTA5MjBfMTcw/MDAxNTY4OTQ0MDc2ODEx.16aUIg6GyfAolrsyA-nxLRWdVP9lsHfbG6nUajcg3hsg.hdmFVKYtq7GLZE7FZTvCa7h_xGci-ysUeBmTTydw8ucg.JPEG.hsback2000/20190918_151226.jpg?type=w800](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAxOTA5MjBfMTcw/MDAxNTY4OTQ0MDc2ODEx.16aUIg6GyfAolrsyA-nxLRWdVP9lsHfbG6nUajcg3hsg.hdmFVKYtq7GLZE7FZTvCa7h_xGci-ysUeBmTTydw8ucg.JPEG.hsback2000/20190918_151226.jpg?type=w800)

The signature items are the sweet red bean bun, filled to the brim with red bean paste, and the vegetable bread, baked with vegetables. I heard people queue in separate lines just to buy these two, so I allowed plenty of time. Parking is not possible there, so I left the car far away and walked.

For this course, bread stands in for lunch. I wonder whether it's right to skip a proper lunch for bread in a post that claims to be about food, but it is bread with nearly a hundred years of history, so please let it slide.

![[이성당]]

### Chowon Photo Studio (초원사진관) | The Photo Studio from the Movie Has Reopened

In a Sinheung-dong alley not far from Isungdang stands Chowon Photo Studio, the filming location of the movie <Christmas in August>.  
The story of Jeong-won (Han Suk-kyu), a photographer with a terminal illness, and Da-rim (Shim Eun-ha), a parking enforcement officer, was filmed here. The crew reportedly skipped building a set and searched for real locations, ending up converting a garage shaded by tree shadows into the photo studio. The building, which was demolished after filming, was restored by Gunsan City and is open to the public for free.

![https://news.kbs.co.kr/data/news/2013/11/20/2758515_QGg.jpg](https://news.kbs.co.kr/data/news/2013/11/20/2758515_QGg.jpg)

Inside, the camera, electric fan, and albums that appeared in the film are displayed just as they were. Although the film came out in 1998, standing in front of the studio still brings the scenes to mind, which shows how memorable the space in the film was.

![[초원사진관]]

### Sinheung-dong Japanese-style House (신흥동 일본식가옥) | A Film Location and a Record of Exploitation

A short walk from Chowon Photo Studio is the Sinheung-dong Japanese-style House, also called the Hirotsu House.  
It is a two-story wooden house built by Hirotsu Keisaburo, a Japanese man who ran a fabric store and a farm during the colonial period, and it was designated a National Registered Cultural Heritage in 2005. Many films, including <Son of the General> and <Tazza: The High Rollers>, were shot here.

![https://i.namu.wiki/i/TVzN0Fls4KWE069DZS_mUcyf3tphvuzwtWHPxU7nCU6yaG_9p8HYzVYSSu2mRc_tO7o1G5q0u7pb7BQ7a_R1Dw.webp](https://i.namu.wiki/i/TVzN0Fls4KWE069DZS_mUcyf3tphvuzwtWHPxU7nCU6yaG_9p8HYzVYSSu2mRc_tO7o1G5q0u7pb7BQ7a_R1Dw.webp)

To protect the cultural heritage, the interior is closed to visitors, so you tour mainly the exterior and the Japanese garden between the two buildings. It carries the label of a film location, but I wanted to remember that this is also a space that shows how a Japanese landowner lived and the history of the exploitation of farming communities.

![[신흥동 일본식가옥]]

### Gyeongam-dong Railroad Village (경암동 철길마을) | An Alley Where Houses Meet the Tracks

![https://access.visitkorea.or.kr/bfvk_img/call?cmd=VIEW&id=1d0e47eb-f543-4163-b926-7ed7e6367a30&](https://access.visitkorea.or.kr/bfvk_img/call?cmd=VIEW&id=1d0e47eb-f543-4163-b926-7ed7e6367a30&)

Next is Gyeongam-dong Railroad Village.  
The village grew up along about 2.5 km of railway laid between Gunsan Station and a factory to carry newsprint during the colonial period. Trains reportedly passed through the village twice a day until 2008, and the houses really are close to the tracks. Along the now-idle tracks, shops offering old-fashioned play experiences, such as making dalgona (honeycomb toffee) and grilling jjondeugi (chewy candy), line both sides.

There is no separate parking lot, and the guidance recommends using the nearby E-Mart Gunsan parking lot. Parking is said to be crowded on weekends and holidays, so please keep that in mind.

![[경암동 철길마을]]

### Samcheon-dong Makgeolli Alley (삼천동 막걸리골목) | One Kettle, a Table Full of Food

It takes about an hour from Gunsan to Jeonju. I dropped off my bags and the car at the accommodation and headed to Samcheon-dong Makgeolli Alley for dinner. (Since I planned to drink, I took a taxi. It goes without saying, but I'm noting it anyway.)

![https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAxODA4MjlfMjIz/MDAxNTM1NDk5NjkyNzU0.CS1Q1wO8YZ-lRebIFlQQ3mQvEkqbblFEN3m5POW5pukg.n8C5SnNAfTrqbvBo7I99J5OERndX9pNpMrgYojmVACYg.JPEG.jcjkks/image_8285561411535499656163.jpg?type=w800](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAxODA4MjlfMjIz/MDAxNTM1NDk5NjkyNzU0.CS1Q1wO8YZ-lRebIFlQQ3mQvEkqbblFEN3m5POW5pukg.n8C5SnNAfTrqbvBo7I99J5OERndX9pNpMrgYojmVACYg.JPEG.jcjkks/image_8285561411535499656163.jpg?type=w800)

This is an alley of old makgeolli taverns packed into about 200 meters around Geomasan-ro, and it is designated a Jeonju Future Heritage site. Its biggest feature is that ordering just one kettle of makgeolli gets you a table filled with more than twenty side dishes, such as grilled fish, jeon (pan-fried pancakes), stew, and fried food. I tried to count whether there really were more than twenty, but gave up halfway.

The atmosphere and the side dish lineup differ a little from shop to shop, and queues sometimes form in the evening. This is how the first day wraps up in Jeonju.

![[삼천동 막걸리골목]]

## [DAY 2] From a Market Morning to a Gomtang Dinner, Passing from Jeonbuk into Jeonnam

Jeonju Nambu Market → Imsil Cheese Theme Park → Sunchang Gochujang Folk Village → Juknokwon → Damyang Metasequoia Road → Naju Gomtang Hayanjip

On the second day, we head south all day. Breakfast in Jeonju, lunch in Sunchang, the afternoon in Damyang, and dinner in Naju. We cross the provincial border only once, going from Sunchang to Damyang.

### Jeonju Nambu Market (전주 남부시장) | Breakfast at the Market

Jeonju Nambu Market is a traditional market that began as the "Nammunbak Market" (the market outside the south gate) in the early Joseon dynasty and was opened as a regular public market in 1905. It has about 800 stalls, and the second floor houses a youth mall run by young merchants.

![https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcQqRvfsfoz4zuifcZ5UbU8xTHCr-XjQ0AF4N4MJpZku9kmHSXj7gzbCcvs&s=10](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcQqRvfsfoz4zuifcZ5UbU8xTHCr-XjQ0AF4N4MJpZku9kmHSXj7gzbCcvs&s=10)

In front of the market, along Jeonjucheon Stream, a Dokkaebi (goblin) Market is said to open at 4–5 a.m. and vanish once day breaks. I wasn't confident I could wake up that early, so I skipped it this time. Instead, I had breakfast at a restaurant in the market and took in the energy of the local vendors and customers. A night market also opens on Fridays and Saturdays, but I had already settled on the makgeolli alley for the first evening, so I left it for another time.

![[전주 남부시장]]

### Imsil Cheese Theme Park (임실치즈테마파크) | The History of Cheese, Started by Father Ji Jeong-hwan

It took less than an hour to get from Jeonju to Imsil.  
Imsil Cheese Theme Park is themed around Imsil cheese, which originated when Father Ji Jeong-hwan, a priest from Belgium, started raising goats and making cheese. It sits on a meadow of about 130,000 ㎡, the size of 19 soccer fields, and has an atmosphere as if you had arrived in a Swiss village. It has a lot of man-made facilities, but with that wide open meadow, I'll count it as nature... heh

![https://img0.yna.co.kr/etc/inner/KR/2017/01/12/AKR20170112156000055_01_i_P2.jpg](https://img0.yna.co.kr/etc/inner/KR/2017/01/12/AKR20170112156000055_01_i_P2.jpg)

By the way, that building... doesn't it look more like Pac-Man than cheese? lol

Anyway.
There are also experience programs such as cheese making and rice pizza making, and the restaurant serves dishes made with cheese produced here. Experiences run at set times, so I recommend checking the schedule on the website before visiting. It is also said to be famous for its chrysanthemum scenery in autumn.

![[임실치즈테마파크]]

### Sunchang Gochujang Folk Village (순창 고추장민속마을) | Lunch with the Flavor of Fermented Sauces

From Imsil we moved on to Sunchang for lunch.  
Sunchang Traditional Gochujang Folk Village is a village of traditional fermented sauces built in hanok style, where artisans make and sell gochujang (chili paste), doenjang (soybean paste), and ganjang (soy sauce) on the spot. At the Sunchang Fermented Sauce Museum in front of the village, you can learn the history and production process of gochujang and doenjang, and at the Sunchang Fermented Sauce Experience Center, you can try activities such as making gochujang.

![https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNTA4MjZfODAg/MDAxNzU2MTkyNDUwNTU1.EegI38btXjxv4cQJjkFLpKZc_efNp0AzQ7cotc9bQ9gg.BEzI0ZK_YYKkIYVm0tewrqtFpK12V4dl7nrSqCcKTA0g.JPEG/%EC%82%AC%EC%A7%841.jpg?type=w800](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNTA4MjZfODAg/MDAxNzU2MTkyNDUwNTU1.EegI38btXjxv4cQJjkFLpKZc_efNp0AzQ7cotc9bQ9gg.BEzI0ZK_YYKkIYVm0tewrqtFpK12V4dl7nrSqCcKTA0g.JPEG/%EC%82%AC%EC%A7%841.jpg?type=w800)

The restaurants in the village serve Korean food made with Sunchang local produce and seasoned with Sunchang gochujang. I had lunch here. I liked that you can see, make, and eat all in one place. It felt efficient.

![[순창 고추장민속마을]]

### Juknokwon (죽녹원) | 2.2 km Through the Bamboo Forest

It took about forty minutes from Sunchang to Damyang. Along this stretch, we leave Jeonbuk and enter Jeonnam.

![https://cdn.erounews.kr/news/photo/202505/390_1289_3231.jpg](https://cdn.erounews.kr/news/photo/202505/390_1289_3231.jpg)

Juknokwon is a bamboo forest of about 160,000 ㎡ near Damyangcheon Stream and Gwanbangjerim. The trail totals 2.2 km and is divided into eight themed paths, including the Unsudaetong Path (Path of Good Fortune), the Jungmagou Path (Path of Childhood Friends), and the Philosopher's Path. Going by name alone, I liked the Philosopher's Path best. I don't know why, though...

From the observatory, you can look down on Damyangcheon Stream, the Gwanbangjerim grove with trees over 300 years old, and even the Metasequoia Road, our next destination. Just walking while listening to the wind rustle through the bamboo made me feel the morning's travel fatigue ease a little.

![[죽녹원]]

### Damyang Metasequoia Road (담양 메타세쿼이아길) | A Scene from Somewhere as You Walk

This is the road we saw from the Juknokwon observatory.

![https://blog.kakaocdn.net/dna/cSwJH0/btr8BehPbfW/AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAiv8giE1CC1UIsrBtFJxLkX2x1gzQREdsQhOH7tjEMR/img.jpg?credential=yqXZFxpELC7KVnFOS48ylbz2pIh7yKj8&expires=1796050799&allow_ip=&allow_referer=&signature=QOnkYqw2ScvlmrCzAyroPNYctBM%3D](https://blog.kakaocdn.net/dna/cSwJH0/btr8BehPbfW/AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAiv8giE1CC1UIsrBtFJxLkX2x1gzQREdsQhOH7tjEMR/img.jpg?credential=yqXZFxpELC7KVnFOS48ylbz2pIh7yKj8&expires=1796050799&allow_ip=&allow_referer=&signature=QOnkYqw2ScvlmrCzAyroPNYctBM%3D)

It is a tree-lined road created in 1972 when Damyang County planted 1,300 five-year-old metasequoia trees along National Route 24, and it has now become a green tunnel reaching for the sky. Stand in the middle of the road and the trunks on both sides form a symmetrical composition, making it a popular photo spot.

It is also known as a filming location for the films <Wanee & Junah (와니와 준하)> and <May 18 (화려한 휴가)> and the variety show <2 Days & 1 Night>. It is a walking path with an admission fee, and its operating hours change with the season, so please check before visiting.

![[담양 메타세쿼이아길]]

### Naju Gomtang Hayanjip (나주곰탕 하얀집) | Makgeolli on Day One, Gomtang on Day Two

It takes about an hour from Damyang to Naju. I aimed to arrive before dinner service ended.

Naju Gomtang Hayanjip is an old establishment that began as "Ryumun Restaurant" in the marketplace of the Naju magistrate's residence in 1910 and has continued for over 110 years; today, fourth-generation master Gil Hyeong-seon carries on the family business. They reportedly make the broth by boiling it for three hours starting at 2 a.m., then skimming off fat and impurities for another three hours. They use Korean beef (hanwoo), and unlike the milky gomtang made by simmering beef bones for a long time, the broth is made mainly from meat such as brisket and shank, which is why Naju gomtang is known for its clear, clean-tasting broth.

![https://d12zq4w4guyljn.cloudfront.net/750_750_20240413110123282_photo_561bc5d6a751.webp](https://d12zq4w4guyljn.cloudfront.net/750_750_20240413110123282_photo_561bc5d6a751.webp)

The signature dishes are the gomtang and the suyuk gomtang, which has more meat. I also liked that it has an open kitchen where you can watch the cooking process, so you can eat with confidence. Queues sometimes form at mealtimes, so please keep that in mind. Makgeolli yesterday, gomtang today: two days in a row of meals accompanied by broth, but it was no problem.

We stay in Naju on the second night.

![[나주곰탕 하얀집]]

## [DAY 3] Past Reeds and Thatched Roofs to the Night Sea of Yeosu

Suncheon Bay Wetland Reserve → Suncheon Drama Filming Site → Nagan Eupseong Folk Village → Nangman Pocha Street

On the last day, we head to the eastern end of Jeonnam. It takes about an hour and a half by car from Naju to Suncheon. We left early in the morning to arrive when the Suncheon Bay Wetland Reserve opens.

### Suncheon Bay Wetland Reserve (순천만습지) | Reed Fields and the S-Shaped Waterway

Suncheon Bay Wetland is a coastal wetland around Suncheon Bay, surrounded by Suncheon city, the Goheung Peninsula, and the Yeosu Peninsula, and is Scenic Site No. 41, known as one of the world's five major coastal wetlands. A reed colony of about 5.4 ㎢ stretches from where the Suncheon Dongcheon and Isacheon streams meet to the front of the tidal flats. Walking along the reed boardwalk and Mujin Bridge, you can see the mudflats and migratory birds up close.

![https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/35/20181231_Suncheon_Bay_002.jpg?utm_source=ko.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=index&utm_content=original](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/35/20181231_Suncheon_Bay_002.jpg?utm_source=ko.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=index&utm_content=original)

From Yongsan Observatory, the S-curve waterway comes into view all at once. It is said to be prettiest around sunset, but since I had planned to spend this evening in Yeosu, I settled for the daytime view. There are also programs like the eco-tour boat and the Suncheon Bay Observatory, but the timing didn't work out, so I skipped them this time. Please be sure to check the operating hours and closing days before visiting.

![[순천만습지]]

### Suncheon Drama Filming Site (순천드라마촬영장) | Into the Alleys of the 1960s–80s

Not far from Suncheon Bay Wetland is the Suncheon Drama Filming Site.  
It is the largest filming site in Korea, recreating Suncheon's town streets of the 1960s, Seoul's Bongcheon-dong hillside neighborhood of the 1970s, and the outskirts of Seoul in the 1980s. More than 80 works have been shot here, including <Love and Ambition>, <Giant>, <East of Eden>, <Baker King Kim Tak-gu>, <Tale of the Nine-Tailed 1938>, and <Pachinko>, and recently <When Life Gives You Tangerines>, <Jeongnyeon: The Star Is Born>, and <Our Unwritten Seoul> were also filmed here.

![https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcTMxHzu657g9u3kZUxxBV3VilrhWzzivo8qd1WQwn6oK8BY91sVk2jEe7jf&s=10](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcTMxHzu657g9u3kZUxxBV3VilrhWzzivo8qd1WQwn6oK8BY91sVk2jEe7jf&s=10)

In the alleys lined along the slope with faded signs and low-roofed houses, scenes from the productions come to mind naturally as you walk. There is also an experience of renting old school uniforms and taking photos. I just looked around. On days when filming is under way, some routes may be restricted, so please keep that in mind.

![[순천드라마촬영장]]

### Nagan Eupseong Folk Village (낙안읍성) | A Fortress Where People Still Live

After lunch, we headed to Nagan Eupseong. It takes about thirty to forty minutes by car from downtown Suncheon.

Nagan Eupseong is a place where the Joseon-era walls, government office, guesthouse, and thatched houses are preserved close to their original state, and it was the first in Korea to be designated a Historic Site as both fortress and village together. A wall about 1,410 m long surrounds the three villages of Dongnae, Namnae, and Seonae, and around 100 households still live inside the fortress today. Since it is a tourist attraction and at the same time someone's home, you end up looking around quietly.

![https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2e/%EB%82%99%EC%95%88%EC%9D%8D%EC%84%B1%EC%A0%84%EA%B2%BD.jpg?utm_source=ko.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=index&utm_content=original](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2e/%EB%82%99%EC%95%88%EC%9D%8D%EC%84%B1%EC%A0%84%EA%B2%BD.jpg?utm_source=ko.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=index&utm_content=original)

It preserves the way ordinary commoners, rather than the yangban aristocracy, lived, so you see modest scenery of thatched roofs, stone walls, wooden porches, and jangdokdae (stands for soy sauce crocks). Walking along the top of the wall gives you a full view of the thatched village below. This was the place where I walked the slowest on this trip.

![[낙안읍성]]

### Nangman Pocha Street (낭만포차거리) | The Last Night Is Yeosu's Night Sea

It took a little over an hour from Nagan Eupseong to Yeosu. The last meal of this trip is at Yeosu's Nangman Pocha Street.

![https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNDA1MDZfOTUg/MDAxNzE0OTYzNzYzOTE0.HBepkhSl7Qo-YHaTTRkaiEYsba8fTDpwoWqCvJO_SzUg.pBviiCenX-fc9bGneCUe3GZkcDumwIN9sheuY8tnvCsg.JPEG/20240313_184342.jpg?type=w800](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNDA1MDZfOTUg/MDAxNzE0OTYzNzYzOTE0.HBepkhSl7Qo-YHaTTRkaiEYsba8fTDpwoWqCvJO_SzUg.pBviiCenX-fc9bGneCUe3GZkcDumwIN9sheuY8tnvCsg.JPEG/20240313_184342.jpg?type=w800)

It is a pocha (street tent bar) street built in the plaza beneath Geobukseon Bridge, where you can enjoy food and drinks while looking at the night view of Yeosu's night sea. Currently 18 pocha stalls operate, reportedly selling everything from seasonal dishes to unusual seafood dishes unique to Yeosu. Busking performances are held even on ordinary days, adding to the atmosphere.

Operating hours differ by season. They reportedly open at 6 p.m. in summer and 5 p.m. in winter, so please check for your time of visit. Visiting nearby Yi Sun-sin Square and Jinnamgwan together is said to make a good evening stroll through Yeosu's old downtown.

![[낭만포차거리]]

## Wrap-up

Over the three days, we kept heading in one direction, south, starting on the west coast and passing through Jeonju, Damyang, and Naju to reach Yeosu. Since there was no route going back up, getting around was relatively easy. In exchange, several places such as Soswaewon, Gwanghalluwon, and Seohak-dong Art Village were left out of the route. I left them out on purpose, because including everything would have turned this into a trip spent entirely on the road. I'll leave them for next time...

Visitor information and operating hours can change, so I recommend checking each place's official notice once more before you set out. Thank you to everyone who read to the end.
