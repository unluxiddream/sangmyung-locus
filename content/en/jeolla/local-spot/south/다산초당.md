Gangjin's Dasan Chodang is where Jeong Yak-yong, a Silhak scholar of the late Joseon Dynasty, was exiled to Gangjin in the Catholic Persecution of 1801 (Sinyu Saok), and stayed from 1808 until his release, spending more than ten years teaching disciples and devoting himself to writing. Major works representing Joseon Silhak, such as Mongmin Simseo (Admonitions on Governing the People) and Gyeongse Yupyo (Design for Good Government), are considered to have been completed during this period. Today, Dongam, where Dasan lived, and Seoam, where his disciples stayed, have been restored, and other traces remain, including 'Jeongseok,' characters he is said to have carved himself into a folding-screen-shaped rock; Yakcheon spring and Dajo, the stone used for brewing tea; Yeonji Seokgasan, a stone mound in the pond; and Cheonilgak Pavilion overlooking Gangjin Bay. Visiting together with the Dasan Museum in Doam-myeon will deepen your understanding of Dasan's life and philosophy. It is one of Namdo's most representative historic sites, free to visit with no admission fee.
