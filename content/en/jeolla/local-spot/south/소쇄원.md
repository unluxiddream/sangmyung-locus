Damyang's Soswaewon is a private garden that the scholar Yang San-bo began creating in the 1530s during the reign of King Jungjong of Joseon; preserving the archetype of the Korean private garden, it has been designated Scenic Site No. 40. Daebongdae, Gwangpunggak and Jewoldang pavilions sit within a space of about 1,400 pyeong (approx. 4,600 ㎡), and a valley stream flowing down from the mountain slope to the north passes beneath the wall and crosses the center of the garden. Soswaewon's greatest charm lies in how, rather than putting the buildings first, it preserves natural elements as they are—bamboo, plum blossoms, pine trees, broad flat rocks and small waterfalls. Although its buildings were burned during the Jeongyu War, descendants restored them, and the family has tended the garden for 15 generations to this day. It represents Damyang's pavilion culture, known as the birthplace of Gasa literature, and smoking is prohibited inside the garden.
