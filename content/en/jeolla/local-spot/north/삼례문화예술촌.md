Samnye Cultural Art Village brings back to life warehouses built in the 1920s under the Japanese rice production expansion plan to ship grain to Japan via Samnye Station, transforming them into cultural spaces. After liberation, the buildings were used as agricultural cooperative (Nonghyup) warehouses until 2010; Wanju-gun then purchased them and developed the site as a regional regeneration project, opening it in June 2013. Exhibition Hall 1 retains its high ceilings and the wooden structure used to stack grain, letting you feel the traces of the old warehouse, while exhibitions, hands-on programs and performances are held in the multipurpose space and outdoor plaza. Nearby is Swieoga Sam[nye:], the old Samnye Station building converted into a travelers' rest area, which is worth visiting together. Here you can discover the story of a space marked by the pain of colonial exploitation that has become a cultural space for local residents.
