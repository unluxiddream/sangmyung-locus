The Mireuksa Temple Site in Iksan is the site of the largest temple of the Baekje Kingdom, built during the reign of King Mu. It is Historic Site No. 150 and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2015 together with the Wanggung-ri site. On the vast temple grounds, measuring about 260 m east to west and about 640 m north to south, stands the Stone Pagoda of Mireuksa Temple Site (National Treasure No. 11); at 14.24 m tall, it is regarded as the largest and oldest stone pagoda in Korea. A restored stone pagoda stands to the east, letting you imagine the original layout of twin pagodas facing each other east and west. Sarira reliquaries were unearthed during the dismantling and repair of the stone pagoda in 2009, and artifacts excavated here can be viewed free of charge at Iksan National Museum on the temple grounds. It is a historic destination where you can stroll leisurely across the open temple precincts and feel the scale of a Baekje temple.
