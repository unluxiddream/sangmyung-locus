Situated at the heart of South Korea, Chungcheong-do is a place for reflection and relaxation, where a rich history, traditional culture, and beautiful natural landscapes come together. Its name combines the first characters of Chungju and Cheongju, two major towns of the Joseon period. The region is also known as Hoseo, meaning 'west of the lake.' Today, the greater Chungcheong region encompasses Chungcheongbuk-do, Chungcheongnam-do, Daejeon Metropolitan City, and Sejong Special Self-Governing City. It is a transportation crossroads linking the Seoul metropolitan area with the Yeongnam and Honam regions, as well as an important center for administration and science.

Chungcheong-do offers striking natural diversity, with majestic mountains, broad plains, and scenic coastlines. Chungcheongbuk-do, South Korea's only landlocked province, is home to Songnisan Mountain and the pristine waterways of the Namhan and Geum rivers. It boasts important cultural treasures such as the UNESCO-listed Beopjusa Temple and Jikji, and is emerging as a hub of biotechnology and advanced industries around Ochang and Osong. Along the west coast, Chungcheongnam-do preserves the splendid legacy of Baekje in the former capitals of Gongju and Buyeo. It is also a major manufacturing center, with semiconductor industries in Cheonan and Asan and petrochemical and steel industries in Dangjin and Seosan.

Chungcheong-do has long been shaped by Confucian traditions and the culture of scholarly gentlemen, giving it a calm and welcoming character. Its famously unhurried dialect and gentle humor reflect the warmth and down-to-earth spirit of local people. Regional specialties such as Cheonan walnut cakes, Gongju chestnuts, Seosan salted oysters, and Danyang garlic, along with festivals including the Baekje Cultural Festival and Boryeong Mud Festival, offer visitors a wealth of experiences. Discover Baekje's history, unspoiled nature, and warm hospitality in Chungcheong-do, and rediscover the pleasure of slowing down.
