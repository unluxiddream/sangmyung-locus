# Namhae Beyond the Sea

Hello, everyone! It's been a while!  
This post is all about my trip to Namhae!  
I'm only just getting around to writing about the trip I took this summer.

This time, I didn't just admire the sea from a distance!  
I learned how people set traps along the paths of flowing seawater,  
heard stories about forests that villagers grew to shield themselves from the wind,  
and even stepped onto the seabed exposed by the receding tide.

I had put together a travel itinerary, too,  
but once I arrived in Namhae, I quickly realized that the tide timetable mattered much more than my carefully planned schedule.

So, shall we get started with my three-day, two-night adventure in Namhae?

## [DAY 1] People Who Read the Flow of Water Rather Than Stop It

Haecheon Anchovy Ssambap → Jukbangnyeom Information Center → Jukbangnyeom Observation Deck → Jijok Banjeom → Goyo Byeolseo

### Haecheon Anchovy Ssambap | A Meal as Soon as We Reached the Sea

I wanted to fuel up before exploring, so our first stop was Haecheon Anchovy Ssambap!

![Haecheon Anchovy Ssambap Photo 1](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/MjAyNTA0MjBfMjgz/MDAxNzQ1MTU5NjE5MzI0.NWH0gko0NI6190BRZRN5GmGDmCNSfR3njm09uZU8eXwg.okx_EjgXrsQDAtp6IHhDycAaotld_Di1fMn8C6UmdCog.JPEG/SE-dbdac82b-bb8e-48d4-889d-5f3ebd196789.jpg?type=w800)

![Haecheon Anchovy Ssambap Photo 2](https://mblogthumb-phinf.pstatic.net/20130618_249/dew36_1371532277663TiDaG_JPEG/IMG_9570.jpg?type=w420)

I had always known anchovies as a stir-fried side dish,  
but seeing them take center stage in a ssam (leaf wrap) really made me feel like I was in Namhae.  
The anchovy ssambap was spicier and richer in flavor than I'd expected.  
If you're sensitive to fishy flavors, try starting with just a little anchovy in your first wrap!

Looking back, I'm so glad we chose this for our first meal.  
After seeing the jukbangnyeom fishing traps a few hours later, I realized the anchovies I'd eaten  
weren't just a local specialty I'd tried as a tourist.

First we enjoyed the food, then we went to discover the waterways that made it possible.  
It was the perfect way to begin this trip.

### Jukbangnyeom Information Center | Learning About Traditional Fishing

![Jukbangnyeom Information Center Photo 1](https://cdn.welfarehello.com/naver-blog/production/namhae_gun/2024-11/223672071108/namhae_gun_223672071108_2.jpg?f=webp&q=80&w=800)

When we arrived at the Jukbangnyeom Information Center,  
the scale model was the first thing that caught my eye.  
Even the exterior made me curious about jukbang anchovies.

It's a place where you can learn about traditional fishing methods!  
At the center, you can explore everything from the history and workings of jukbangnyeom  
to the story of the anchovies caught in these traps.

![Jukbangnyeom Information Center Photo 2](https://vbfiadwc1rwulbsfawoq15381.edge.naverncp.com/german_village/nearby_tourist/images/HCH_4895.jpg)

Jukbangnyeom is a traditional fishing method that makes use of Namhae's fast-flowing tidal currents.  
Fishers install fences made of bamboo and oak in the sea,  
guiding fish carried in by the current into a naturally enclosed space.  
Rather than relying on artificial force, the method works by understanding the direction and speed of the water  
and making use of the sea's natural flow!

![Jukbangnyeom Information Center Photo 3](https://german-village.kr/_next/image?url=https%3A%2F%2Fvbfiadwc1rwulbsfawoq15381.edge.naverncp.com%2Fgerman_village%2Fnearby_tourist%2Fimages%2FHCH_4865.jpg&w=3840&q=100)

There was even a 4D jukbangnyeom experience zone, so there wasn't a dull moment!

**📍 Jukbangnyeom Information Center | Visitor Information**

- **Opening hours:** 09:00–18:00

This restaurant is one of the main filming locations for the movie 'If We'.  
In the film, it appears under the name Eunho Restaurant

The tangsuyuk comes with the sauce already poured over it.  
Even with the sauce, it was crispy on the outside and tender inside!  
I especially liked the thick pieces of meat and their satisfying texture.

### Mulgeon-ri Coastal Protection Forest | The Village's Breakwater Was Made of Trees

After breakfast, we headed to the Mulgeon-ri Coastal Protection Forest.  
This time, we were going to see a forest instead of the sea!

![Mulgeon-ri Coastal Protection Forest Photo 1](https://img.thetravelnews.co.kr/2026/08/namhae-mulgeon-windbreak-forest-aerial-01.png)

A coastal protection forest is a planted woodland that helps prevent seawater from flooding inland  
and protects nearby villages.  
A fish-attracting forest, meanwhile, creates conditions suitable for marine life  
and helps draw schools of fish toward the shore.

Because this forest serves both purposes,  
it is known in Korean as a *bangjo-eoburim*, a coastal protection and fish-attracting forest!

![Mulgeon-ri Coastal Protection Forest Photo 2](https://img.thetravelnews.co.kr/2026/08/namhae-mulgeon-windbreak-forest-trail-02.png)

In the late 19th century, part of the forest was cut down,  
and a storm reportedly caused severe damage to the village that same year.  
Since then, people have passed down the saying that harming the forest would bring ruin to the village,  
and they have continued to protect it to this day.

![Mulgeon-ri Coastal Protection Forest Photo 3](https://cdn.welfarehello.com/naver-blog/production/namhae_gun/2023-06/223134938622/namhae_gun_223134938622_0.jpg)

Even though it was the middle of a blazing hot summer,  
it felt cool inside the forest.  
It was the perfect place to put aside busy thoughts for a while  
and enjoy a leisurely walk.

### Sangju Silver Sand Beach | Looking at the Sea Again at the End of the Journey

On our last day in Namhae,  
we decided to take things a little slower and enjoy the ocean.
That's how we ended up at Sangju Silver Sand Beach!

![Sangju Silver Sand Beach Photo 1](https://cdn.welfarehello.com/naver-blog/production/namhae_gun/2024-10/223634151536/namhae_gun_223634151536_0.jpg?f=webp&q=80&w=800)

I took off my shoes and walked slowly across the sand.  
The cool sound of the waves left me feeling refreshed.

![Sangju Silver Sand Beach Photo 3](https://www.telltrip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/namhae-sangju-eunmorae-beach-newtro-travel-1.webp)

and then retreat.  
It occurred to me that the currents we'd seen at the jukbangnyeom traps on Day 1  
and the stories we'd heard in the fishing village on Day 2  
were all connected to the movement of this same sea.

When I first arrived in Namhae,  
I thought I'd come simply to see its beautiful ocean.  
But by the final day, I felt like I was looking at the same sea in a slightly different way.

There are plenty of cafés near Sangju Silver Sand Beach,  
so if you have some time, it's a nice place to relax with a drink.

## On the Way Home: What I Want to Take Away from Namhae

And that's the end of our three-day, two-night Namhae trip!  
I discovered so many sides of Namhae that I had never really known before.

At the jukbangnyeom traps, I learned about traditional fishing methods,  
and at the Mulgeon-ri Coastal Protection Forest, I heard how villagers had protected their homes from the sea.  
Most of all, I'll remember stepping into the tidal flats and digging for shellfish myself.
There's definitely a difference between simply admiring the ocean and experiencing it firsthand!  
I also loved the peaceful time I spent listening to the waves at Sangju Silver Sand Beach on our final day.

Throughout the trip, we enjoyed lots of delicious food  
and had plenty of rest at our quiet hanok accommodation,  
giving us a welcome break from everyday life.

At first, I thought I was going on a trip simply to see the beautiful sea.  
But on the way home, I found myself remembering not only Namhae's natural scenery  
but also the wisdom of the people who make their lives here.

It was a short but wonderfully full three-day, two-night journey!  
Next time I visit Namhae, I'd love to take my time exploring the places we didn't get to see this time.

And with that, I'll wrap up today's Namhae travel post.  
Thanks so much for reading!
