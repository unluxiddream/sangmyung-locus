Q. Is Google Maps enough for traveling in South Korea?
A. Some navigation features in Google Maps may be limited in South Korea or less detailed than local map apps. We recommend using Naver Map or KakaoMap as well. Both provide public transit routes, walking directions, and information about nearby facilities, with English-language features for international travelers.

Q. Can I travel without a car?
A. Yes. Each attraction's detail page has a public transit section describing how to get there from a nearby train station or bus terminal. In general, take a train or intercity bus to the main town, then transfer to a city bus, rural bus, or taxi. Some rural routes operate infrequently, so check timetables in advance and group nearby attractions into the same itinerary.

Q. Where can I check real-time bus information?
A. Naver Map and KakaoMap show bus routes and estimated arrival times. KakaoBus is also useful for real-time bus updates. However, live data is not available in every region, so check the published timetable when using rural buses.

Q. How do I ride buses and pay the fare?
A. Transportation cards such as T-money make it easy to ride most buses and subways in South Korea. You can buy a transit card at convenience stores and some transport facilities. Tap the card on the reader when boarding and again when getting off to receive eligible transfer discounts. Some areas restrict cash payments, so we recommend getting a transit card in advance.

Q. Where can I get help if I become ill or get lost while traveling?
A. Call 119 in an emergency. For directions or interpretation, use the 1330 Korea Travel Hotline. Tourist information centers near major attractions and transport hubs can also provide maps and public transit information. Since medical facilities and public health centers may have different operating hours, check before visiting.

Q. Where can I buy medicine if I feel unwell during my trip?
A. Over-the-counter medicines such as cold remedies, digestive aids, and pain relievers are available at pharmacies. Some convenience stores also sell designated emergency medicines. However, pharmacies may close in the evening or on weekends, so check their hours in advance. If you take regular medication, bring enough for your trip.

Q. Do I need to tip in South Korea?
A. Tipping is not customary in South Korea. At restaurants, cafes, and in taxis, simply pay the stated price; there is no need to leave an additional tip.

Q. Can I pay with a foreign-issued card or withdraw cash?
A. Foreign-issued cards are widely accepted at hotels, major retailers, and chain businesses. Some small restaurants and traditional markets may not accept them, so carrying a little cash is recommended. You can withdraw cash at ATMs marked “Global ATM” in banks or convenience stores. Availability and fees vary by card type.

Q. Is tap water safe to drink in South Korea?
A. South Korean tap water is regulated under strict water quality standards and is generally safe to drink. However, water quality can vary depending on building pipes, and many people prefer filtered or bottled water. Most restaurants provide drinking water free of charge, sometimes at a self-service station.

Q. Can I dine alone at restaurants?
A. Yes, most restaurants welcome solo diners. Some establishments, however, require a minimum order for two people. If traveling alone, try dishes commonly sold as single portions, such as gukbap (rice soup), bibimbap, and noodles.

Q. What types of power outlets are used in South Korea?
A. South Korea uses 220V electricity at 60Hz, primarily with Type C and Type F sockets featuring two round pins. Bring a travel adapter if your device uses a different plug. A separate voltage converter may be necessary for devices that do not support the local voltage.
