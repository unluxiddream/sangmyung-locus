[Transportation and Directions]
Q. How can I find directions and check bus service information when traveling in Korea?
A. In Korea, some navigation features in Google Maps may be limited or less detailed than those in local map apps, so it is a good idea to use Naver Map or KakaoMap as well. Both apps provide public transportation routes, walking directions, nearby facilities, bus routes, and estimated arrival times. They also offer English-language support for international travelers. You can also check real-time bus information using the KakaoBus app. However, real-time information may not be available in every area, so please check separate bus timetables, especially when using rural buses.

Q. Can I visit local tourist attractions without a car?
A. Yes, you can. You can find directions from nearby train stations or bus terminals in the “Public Transportation” section of each attraction's detail page. Generally, you can travel to major areas by train or intercity bus and then take a city bus, rural bus, or taxi. However, some areas have infrequent bus services, so it is best to check the timetable before departure and plan to visit attractions that are close to one another.

Q. How do I use buses and pay fares in Korea?
A. Transportation cards such as T-money make it convenient to use most buses and subways. You can purchase these cards at convenience stores or certain transportation facilities. Tap your card on the reader when boarding the bus, and tap it again when getting off to receive benefits such as transfer discounts. As cash payments are restricted in some areas, we recommend preparing a transportation card in advance.

Q. Are there places where I can rent a car?
A. Yes. There are car rental companies in the town centers near Yeongdong Station and Okcheon Station in Chungcheong Province. However, there may be fewer companies and vehicle options than in large cities, so we recommend booking in advance. International travelers should prepare the necessary documents, such as an International Driving Permit and passport, beforehand.

[Tourist Attractions and Accommodation Facilities]
Q. Can I store my luggage before check-in or at tourist attractions?
A. Many accommodations offer luggage storage before check-in, but availability varies by property, so it is best to ask in advance. If your accommodation cannot store your luggage, you may be able to use lockers at certain tourist information centers, train stations, bus terminals, or tourist facilities. These services are not available at every attraction, and their operating hours and fees vary, so please check with the facility or tourist information center before visiting.

Q. Are there tourist attractions I can visit with my pet?
A. Some tourist attractions, parks, walking trails, and outdoor facilities allow pets, but entry may be restricted at places such as museums, exhibition halls, and accommodations. Before visiting, check whether pets are allowed and review the rules. Please also follow basic pet etiquette, such as keeping your pet on a leash and cleaning up after it. You can also find information about pet-friendly attractions on [Meongnyang Map](https://mkt.naver.com/puppymap).

Q. Are there tourist attractions accessible by wheelchair or stroller?
A. Some tourist attractions are accessible by wheelchair or stroller, and some have facilities such as ramps and elevators. However, access may be limited by the terrain or facility conditions, so please check the available accessibility facilities and access conditions before visiting. You can also find related information on the [Yeollin Tourism](https://access.visitkorea.or.kr/ms/list.do) website.

[Restaurants and Food Culture]
Q. Can I dine alone at restaurants?
A. Yes. You can dine alone at most restaurants. However, some restaurants may require an order of at least two servings. If you are traveling alone, we recommend dishes available in single servings, such as gukbap (rice soup), bibimbap (mixed rice), and noodles.

Q. Are side dishes provided free of charge at Korean restaurants?
A. Yes. At most Korean restaurants, basic side dishes are served with your main dish. Many restaurants also offer free refills of side dishes, but some may charge extra, so please check with the staff.

Q. Do I need to tip at restaurants or cafés in Korea?
A. No. Tipping is generally not customary in Korea. At restaurants, cafés, taxis, and similar services, you only need to pay the stated price, and there is no need to leave an additional tip.

Q. Is tap water safe to drink in Korea?
A. Tap water in Korea is managed under strict water quality standards and is generally safe to drink. However, water quality may vary depending on factors such as the condition of a building's pipes, so many people choose filtered or bottled water. Most restaurants provide drinking water free of charge, and some have self-service areas where you can get water yourself.

[Payments and Cash]
Q. Can I pay by card at traditional markets? Can I also use a card issued overseas?
A. Most traditional markets accept credit and debit cards. Cards issued overseas are also often accepted at hotels, large stores, and franchise businesses. However, some small market stalls, independently owned shops, and restaurants may not accept cards or foreign-issued cards, and some may accept only cash or bank transfers, so it is a good idea to carry a small amount of cash. You can withdraw cash at ATMs marked “Global ATM” in places such as banks and convenience stores. Availability and fees may vary depending on your card.

[Health and Emergencies]
Q. Where can I ask for help if I get sick or lost while traveling?
A. In an emergency, call 119. If you need directions or interpretation assistance, you can call the 1330 Korea Travel Helpline. Tourist information centers near major attractions and transportation facilities can also provide maps and transportation information. Medical facilities and public health centers may have different operating hours, so it is best to check before visiting.

Q. Where can I buy medicine while traveling?
A. Over-the-counter medicines, such as cold remedies, digestive medicines, and pain relievers, are available at pharmacies. Some convenience stores also sell designated over-the-counter emergency medicines. Some pharmacies are closed in the evenings or on weekends, so check their opening hours in advance and bring a sufficient supply of any medications you regularly take.

[Electricity and Mobile Phone Use]
Q. What types of electrical outlets are used in Korea?
A. Korea uses 220V, 60Hz electricity, and mainly uses Type C and Type F outlets with two round pins. If your plug has a different shape, bring a travel plug adapter. Devices that are not compatible with the voltage may require a separate voltage converter.

Q. Can I charge my phone at indoor facilities such as restaurants or cafés?
A. Many restaurants and cafés in Korea allow customers to charge their phones using outlets inside the premises. However, outlet use may be restricted at some places, so please ask the staff first. Even if you cannot see an outlet, you can ask whether phone charging is available.
